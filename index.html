Technology dependency

Behavioral/psychological dependency: Compulsive use of social media, gaming, online shopping, pornography, or general internet use, often accompanied by withdrawal-like symptoms and impaired functioning.

Understanding how technology dependency can have a effect on our social and mental life

  • Sleep Disruption: Prolonged use of digital devices can interfere with sleep patterns, leading to chronic sleep debt and other health issues.
  • Cognitive Impact: Technology can disrupt cognitive functions, such as memory and concentration, due to the need to switch between tasks frequently.
  • Physical Health: Excessive screen time can cause musculoskeletal issues and digital eye strain, which are common among users.
  • Mental Health: Technology can both support mental health and contribute to mental health issues, depending on how it is used. It's important to balance technology use with healthy habits to mitigate these effects and maintain overall well-being.

List of the most common technological dependencies:

  • Social media and instant messaging: People can spend too much time on social media and instant messaging, reducing the time they spend in the real world. Moreover, it leads to a dependency on constant updates of news.
  • Smartphones and tablets: Smartphones and tablets can become the main way to communicate with the world, and people can become addicted to constantly checking notifications, retweeting, and browsing social media and instant messaging.
  • Online gaming and entertainment: Online gaming and entertainment can be addictive, causing people to spend too much time in the virtual world instead of in real life.
  • Online shopping: People can become addicted to online shopping, especially if they use it to meet their emotional needs.

How can we fight technological dependencies?

  • Set limits on the time you spend online/on your devices;
  • Diversify your interests and activities beyond the use of technology;
  • Look for new ways to connect with the world around you to reduce your dependence on social media and instant messaging;
  • Try to limit the time you spend working on the computer and take breaks (if possible). This will reduce eye strain and help clear your mind before your next work session
